## Overview

This epidemiological study investigates the relationship between social determinants of health and COVID-19 mortality across U.S. counties, revealing significant disparities tied to socioeconomic factors, healthcare access, and demographic characteristics.

## Your Contribution

As a data scientist on this applied epidemiology project, I:
- **Data Cleaning & Preparation**: Curated and cleaned county-level data from multiple sources (CDC, Census Bureau, healthcare registries)
- **Exploratory Data Analysis**: Conducted comprehensive EDA to identify patterns and anomalies in mortality data
- **Statistical Analysis**: Designed and executed experiments examining relationships between social factors and mortality outcomes
- **Visualization**: Generated publication-quality figures and maps illustrating geographic disparities
- **Storytelling**: Translated statistical findings into compelling visual narratives for public health audiences

This work demonstrates the power of data-driven approaches in understanding and addressing health equity issues.

## Key Findings

- Social determinants explain significant variance in county-level COVID mortality
- Areas with lower socioeconomic status and reduced healthcare access experienced disproportionate mortality
- Geographic clustering reveals regional health disparities
- Multiple factors interact in complex ways to shape outcomes

## Impact

This work contributes to growing evidence that addressing underlying social determinants is critical for pandemic response and health equity. The findings support policy interventions targeting communities most vulnerable to infectious disease.
